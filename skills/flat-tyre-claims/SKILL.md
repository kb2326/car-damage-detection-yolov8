1. A flat tyre on its own is usually a puncture or wear, not collision damage. Wear and tear is
   excluded in every wording (BAS-8.3, STD-8.3, PRM-8.3).
2. Read the story: a flat tyre fits a collision when the car hit a kerb, a pothole edge or an
   object with that wheel. Look for those words in the customer's account or intake facts.
3. If the story names a kerb or an object hit by the wheel and the estimate is small, the usual
   checks apply.
4. If the story is generic ("low-speed collision") with no mention of the wheel, or the
   estimate is close to the deductible, recommend ADJUSTER_REVIEW and ask whether the flat was
   caused by the collision.
5. Premium includes roadside help for a flat tyre (PRM-7.1); that is a service, not a reason to
   fast-track a damage claim.
6. Cite the wear-and-tear clause when you rely on it.
7. Never call it a puncture as fact; say it could be one and ask.
